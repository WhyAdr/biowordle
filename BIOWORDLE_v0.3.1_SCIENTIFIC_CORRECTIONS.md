# BioWordle v0.3.1 — Scientific corrections

Audit date: 2026-11-14. Baseline: `83f05f96102abea5d2fcce0ed75dbf3535127f71`. This evidence records the proposed corrections; it does not certify a release or migration by itself. No additional compounds are proposed.

## Method and limits

The seven `baseline_legacy` input structures and metadata in `data/chemle_compounds.json` were compared against the named PubChem records below; ChEBI is an additional identity/ontology source where cited. PubChem and ChEBI are not necessarily independent primary experimental determinations, and PubChem name/synonym aggregation is not independent proof of structure. RDKit parsed the source SMILES and independently calculated formula, formal charge, canonical isomeric SMILES, and standard InChIKey locally. The target keys below reproduced the cited PubChem keys. Standard InChI can normalize certain resonance and mobile-hydrogen representations; matching it alone cannot prove a chosen tautomer or all stereochemical details. The neutral molecular forms are an explicit game representation, not a claim about dominant protonation in a biological environment. No experimental assay or original isolation structure determination was independently reviewed.

## Resolution of source disagreements

1. **Prodigiosin `(5Z)` vs `(5E)` methine geometry:** Earlier audit drafts provisionally selected `(5E)` [PubChem CID 172897611](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/172897611) (`SZXDNGVQRDTJSD-FOWTUZBSSA-N`), whose deposited CID page simultaneously carried a `(Z)` IUPAC synonym (`CHEBI:82758`). Cross-checking [ChEBI:82758](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chebi/CHEBI:82758), [PubChem CID 153274519](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/153274519) (`SZXDNGVQRDTJSD-VBKFSLOCSA-N`), KEGG `C21565`, and NMR/conformational literature (e.g., PMC4106694, PMC12707358) supports `(5Z)` as the reference geometry placing Ring C's basic imine nitrogen inward toward the intramolecular H-bonded tripyrrole pocket (while noting that free-base prodigiosin exhibits dynamic `(Z)/(E)` and syn/anti conformational equilibria in solution).
2. **Violacein `(3E)` dilactam vs acyl-imine/hydroxypyrrole tautomer:** Earlier drafts provisionally used [PubChem CID 11053](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/11053) (`SHLJIZCPRXXHHZ-UHFFFAOYSA-N`), which lacked the `(3E)` double-bond stereolayer and caused RDKit to drop `Amide / Lactam`. Inspecting [ChEBI:131914](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chebi/CHEBI:131914)'s `standard_inchi` (`XAPNKXIRQFHCHN-QGOAFFKASA-N`), KEGG `C21136`, and [PubChem CID 9928039](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/9928039) shows all three agree on `(3E)-3-[5-(5-hydroxy-1H-indol-3-yl)-2-oxo-1,2-dihydro-3H-pyrrol-3-ylidene]-1,3-dihydro-2H-indol-2-one` (the ChEBI web/REST string `n` without `[nH]` was an aromatic SMILES serialization omission corrected by its own standard InChI and KEGG MOL). Adopting the `(3E)` dilactam resolves both stereochemistry and functional-group classification.

## Implemented cutover and functional groups

The schedule identity remains based on the Ranked answer roster; a separate `chemle_rules_hash` identifies playable structures, derived feedback inputs, clue ladders, fingerprint and standardization specifications, the similarity matrix and feedback configuration. `release_version` identifies the release and is not the schedule or rules hash. Changes to the feedback algorithms themselves require incrementing the explicitly maintained engine revision in the rules-hash function. The new Chemle daily/seed keys include both schedule and rules hashes. Old payloads are kept under their original keys and archived, rather than replayed with the corrected structures; the current session starts fresh. Existing ledger and wins remain separate. Old bundles remain necessary to reconstruct historical feedback and play an unfinished historical session; the archive notice preserves the stored record but does not recreate historical feedback. A previously open old tab can keep writing its old key. Hosting must publish matched assets and retain old bundles if historical interactive replay is required.

The full SMARTS catalog has positive and negative RDKit fixtures. The phenol/enol expression now requires an aromatic carbon or a carbon-carbon double bond, excluding carboxylic acid hydroxyls; ester and acid matching exclude carbonate/carbamate examples, and amide and amine patterns distinguish carbonyl- and sulfonyl-bound nitrogen. These are explicitly graph-based teaching labels: acid detection includes carboxylate, sulfur includes all sulfur-containing groups, and halogen includes iodine. They do not predict protonation, biological conditions, or functional activity.
